# Product Marketing Framing Skill

This skill enforces Cloverleaf AI's product marketing frameworks across all PMM outputs. It is **strongly opinionated** — always apply the relevant pillars and structures. Do not default to generic marketing copy.

---

## The Market Thesis (Always True — Never Contradict This)

**Government deals are won long before the RFP.**

Traditional GTM systems were built for commercial sales motions — not the hidden, relationship-driven realities of B2G procurement. The real buying signals live inside public meetings, committees, budget discussions, and stakeholder conversations that happen months or years before formal procurement begins.

Cloverleaf AI transforms fragmented public-sector activity into actionable intelligence so sellers can **shape opportunities early** instead of **reacting late**.

### The Strategic Belief (4 Shifts)
Every piece of content should reflect at least one of these market shifts:

| Old Way | New Way |
|---------|---------|
| Reactive | Proactive |
| Database-led | Intelligence-led |
| Mass outreach | Territory-aware |
| Bid-driven | Relationship-driven |

### The Enemy (What We're Against)
The broken status quo Cloverleaf AI replaces:
- RFP scraping
- Generic intent tools
- Noisy, undifferentiated alerts
- Reactive workflows
- Spray-and-pray outbound

These systems create **information overload without contextual intelligence**. Name this enemy in top-of-funnel and awareness content.

### The Aspiration (What Top B2G Sellers Do)
- Identify deals before competitors
- Influence procurement early
- Understand stakeholder language
- Objection-handle proactively
- Shape the RFP instead of answering it

## Core Narrative Spine: Pain → Impact → Solution

Every piece of content — regardless of format or length — must be anchored in this structure:

1. **Pain** — Name the specific, felt problem the persona experiences. Be concrete. Use their language.
2. **Impact** — Articulate the downstream consequence. What does it cost them? (Lost deals, missed quota, wasted pipeline time, forecast variance, deal slippage, late-stage surprises.)
3. **Solution** — Introduce Cloverleaf AI as the answer. Lead with outcome, not feature. Show the before/after delta.

> Never skip Pain → Impact. Starting with Solution produces feature-first copy that doesn't land.

### Pillar 1 — Full-Funnel Visibility

## The 7 Pillars

Apply all relevant pillars for every task. For long-form outputs (messaging docs, launch narratives), address all seven. For short-form (one-liners, email snippets), collapse to the most critical 2–3.

### 1. Problem Framing
- State the problem from the persona's POV, in their language
- Avoid Cloverleaf-centric framing in the opening — earn the right to introduce the product
- Anchor in the B2G context: procurement timelines, committee dynamics, public-sector buying cycles
- Specificity beats generality: "reps are answering RFPs they had no hand in shaping" beats "sellers face pipeline challenges"

### 2. Objection Handling
Always pre-empt the known objections per persona. Structure responses as:
- **Acknowledge** the concern directly (never minimize)
- **Reframe** using evidence, logic, or the market thesis
- **Bridge** to the Cloverleaf AI advantage

### 3. Persona Logic
Write to one persona at a time. Shared copy loses both. See Persona Reference below.

### 4. Category Language
Cloverleaf AI is **Full-Funnel B2G Sales Intelligence** — purpose-built for public sector. It does not compete in generic sales intelligence, procurement databases, intent data, or public-record search.

**Approved category language:**

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| Full-funnel B2G sales intelligence | Generic sales intelligence |
| Pre-RFP opportunity intelligence | Procurement database |
| Territory-specific government intelligence | Intent data |
| Continuous monitoring of public-sector buying signals | Public-record search |
| Workflow-integrated GTM insights | Another dashboard |
| Shape the deal / shape the RFP | React to the RFP |
| Committee-driven buying intelligence | Lead generation tool |
| Relationship-driven selling | CRM feature |

When introducing Cloverleaf AI for the first time in a piece, anchor the category before naming the product.

### 5. Competitive Positioning
Choose the right mode for context — do not mix modes in a single piece:

| Mode | When to use | Approach |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Category creation** | Top-of-funnel, awareness, brand | Establish Full-Funnel B2G Sales Intelligence as a new category — contrast with RFP scraping, generic intent tools, procurement databases |
| **Comparative (unnamed)** | Mid-funnel, web copy, enablement | "Unlike tools that only activate at procurement..." — contrast without naming |
| **Head-to-head** | Late-stage sales, battlecards, direct comparison | Name the competitor, use specific differentiators backed by evidence |

**The existing market's failure modes** (use for comparative unnamed mode):
- Too horizontal — built for commercial, not B2G
- Too bottom-funnel — activates at RFP, not before
- Too noisy — alerts without context
- Disconnected from how government buying actually works

### 6. Narrative Hierarchy
Structure content so the most important idea comes first:
1. The stakes — the market shift (proactive vs. reactive)
2. The enemy — the broken old way
3. The problem — Pain → Impact for this persona
4. The alternative — Cloverleaf AI + relevant pillar
5. Proof / specificity
6. Call to action or next step

For sales enablement: lead with the business outcome, not the product capability.

### 7. Pain → Impact → Solution Structure
Applied at the paragraph and section level, not just the overall piece. Every major claim should be traceable back to a pain a persona feels and an impact they suffer.

## Persona Reference

### Sales Leadership
**Title signals:** VP of Sales, CRO, Head of Sales, Sales Director, Regional VP

**What they care about:**
- Hitting number / quota attainment
- Forecasting accuracy and pipeline predictability
- Managing and developing talent
- Revenue-generating activity vs. admin overhead
- Unblocking deals and pipeline coverage QoQ
- Sales enablement ROI and qualification hygiene
- Team performance management

**B2G-specific angle:** Managing a team working long, opaque procurement cycles. They need reps positioned early — not scrambling at RFP time. Forecast variance often comes from deals that were never shapeable because the team entered too late.

**Language that lands:** "hit your number", "pipeline coverage", "forecast confidence", "rep productivity", "shape deals before procurement", "territory prioritization", "win before the RFP drops", "coaching that moves the needle"

**Primary objection:** *"Are the insights specific enough to actually act on?"*
Acknowledge they've seen generic tools surface noise → reframe: Cloverleaf signals tie to real government activity (committee discussions, budget language, stakeholder moves), configured to their territory and solution → bridge: every insight is tied to a specific agency, stakeholder, or procurement moment, never a generic alert.

---

### Sales Operations / GTM Operations
**Title signals:** Sales Ops, Rev Ops, GTM Ops, Director of Sales Enablement, Head of Operations

**What they care about:**
- Enabling both leadership and field to hit goals
- Managing the GTM tech stack and reducing tool sprawl
- CRM integration and workflow continuity
- Budget justification, CAC management, and time to value
- Adoption rates and seat utilization
- Reducing tech debt; working cross-functionally with Finance and IT

**B2G-specific angle:** Building operational infrastructure for a sales motion that doesn't fit commercial GTM playbooks. Long cycles, complex stakeholder maps, and geographically constrained TAMs require different tooling logic.

**Language that lands:** "integrates with your CRM", "time to value", "consolidate your stack", "measurable ROI", "workflow-native", "reduces admin burden", "built for B2G motion"

**Primary objections** (Acknowledge → Reframe → Bridge):

1. *"We're trying to slim down the tech stack, not add more point solutions."*
   Unused SaaS seats are a real concern → Cloverleaf replaces the patchwork of RFP scrapers, alert tools, and manual research; it consolidates, it doesn't add to the pile → one workflow-native layer instead of three disconnected tools.
2. *"Is the data complete enough to be reliable?"*
   Stale data makes any insight tool useless → Cloverleaf continuously monitors 70,000+ agencies (meetings, committees, budgets, stakeholder activity), not static records → coverage and freshness are core to the product.
3. *"How does this integrate with our CRM?"*
   Address directly with specific integration capabilities. If unknown, flag it as a question to answer before writing that content.

---

### Account Executive (AE)
**Title signals:** Account Executive, AE, Senior AE, Enterprise AE, Strategic AE

**What they care about:**
- Hitting personal quota and win rate
- Territory time management — knowing where to focus
- Shaping the deal on their terms, not reacting to RFPs and procurement timelines
- Navigating complex government procurement processes
- Account and opportunity prioritization
- Timing outreach and follow-up to land at the right moment
- Having a unique, differentiated point of view when engaging an account
- Increasing average deal size
- Knowing who actually has buying power inside an account

**B2G-specific angle:** The AE who gets in early wins. The AE who shows up at RFP time is competing on price with four other vendors and answering requirements they had no hand in writing.

**Language that lands:** "win rate", "shape the deal", "right account at the right time", "know the room before you walk in", "don't waste a call", "unique angle", "deal size", "get in before the RFP", "procurement maze", "champion vs. blocker", "stakeholder map"

**Primary objections** (Acknowledge → Reframe → Bridge):

1. *"I need insights tailored to my specific deals — not generic advice."*
   Generic alerts are noise → Cloverleaf is configured to their agencies, stakeholders, and solution relevance, not a broad feed → the insight is always territory- and person-specific.
2. *"High-quality insights matter more than volume — I don't need more noise."*
   More data without prioritization makes it worse → Cloverleaf surfaces the actionable signal for this agency at this moment, not a firehose → specificity is the product.
3. *"Setup seems complicated — I don't have time for another onboarding."*
   Every tool promises easy setup and ships a three-week project → Cloverleaf activates on territory/agency configuration, not a new data-collection burden → configure your territory, start getting signals; time to first insight is fast.
4. *"I don't have budget authority — and I don't know who does."*
   AEs are often the champion, not the buyer → equip them with ROI language, exec-level framing, and Sales Ops objection handles → help them name the likely buyer (Sales Ops, RevOps, or Sales Leadership) and the narrative for that conversation.
5. *"I can already do this with ChatGPT."*
   ChatGPT is genuinely useful for drafting — don't dismiss it → but it works on public info and generic prompts; Cloverleaf monitors real government activity (committee meetings, budgets, stakeholder moves) across 70,000+ agencies, data not in ChatGPT's training set → ChatGPT helps you write the email; Cloverleaf tells you when to send it, to whom, and what changed since last quarter.

## Output Formats

### Messaging Doc / Brief
1. One-line category statement (Full-Funnel B2G Sales Intelligence)
2. Market thesis statement (deals won before the RFP)
3. Primary audience + their core pain
4. Pain → Impact → Solution (3 sentences max each)
5. Three proof points or differentiators tied to the 4 solution pillars
6. Objection pre-empts (one per primary persona)
7. Calls to action by persona

### Sales Enablement (One-Pager, Battle Card, Talk Track)
1. Headline using transformation language (e.g., "Reactive Seller → Strategic Advisor")
2. Problem framing in persona language — anchor in B2G context
3. Pain → Impact → Solution
4. The enemy / broken status quo (choose competitive mode)
5. Differentiators tied to relevant solution pillars
6. Objection handling section
7. Proof / specificity (70,000+ agencies, continuous monitoring, territory configuration)
8. Next step / CTA

### Website / Landing Page Copy
1. Hero: outcome-first headline using market thesis or transformation language
2. The enemy section: name the broken old way
3. Problem section: Pain → Impact (no product yet)
4. Solution introduction: category frame → product
5. The 4 pillars as feature sections (Visibility → Execution → Insights → Outreach)
6. Proof section
7. CTA

### Launch Narrative / Announcement
1. The market moment — why B2G selling is broken right now
2. The strategic belief — the 4 shifts
3. What Cloverleaf AI is doing about it
4. What's new / what's launching
5. Customer or market validation
6. What it means for the B2G seller

## Common Failure Modes to Avoid

- **Feature-first copy**: Never open with what the product does — open with what the persona feels
- **Missing the B2G context**: Don't write generic sales intelligence copy. Always anchor in government procurement, public-sector buying cycles, and the pre-RFP opportunity
- **Skipping the enemy**: The broken old way (RFP scraping, generic intent tools, reactive workflows) is part of every top-of-funnel narrative — name it
- **Generic persona**: "Sales teams" is not a persona. Write to Sales Leadership, Sales Ops, or AE specifically
- **Skipping Impact**: Pain without Impact is just a complaint. Always show the downstream cost
- **Mixed competitive modes**: Don't name a competitor and use "unlike tools that..." in the same section
- **Jargon as positioning**: "AI-powered", "data-driven", "holistic" — replace with specifics (70,000+ agencies, committee-level monitoring, territory-configured alerts)
- **Bottom-funnel framing**: Never position Cloverleaf AI as a procurement tool or RFP response tool. It activates before procurement. That is the entire point.
