# Cloverleaf AI Copy Editor

A skill for editing marketing copy to match Cloverleaf AI's brand voice, grammar rules, and punctuation standards.

---

## Brand Voice

**Persona**: A confident, insider peer — not a vendor pitching, not a teacher explaining. The reader is a B2G sales professional. Speak to them as one.

**Tone attributes**:
- Direct and confident. No hedging, no qualifiers like "might", "could potentially", "we believe"
- Urgency without alarm — strategic, not breathless
- Occasionally self-aware or wry, when it earns it ("We know… it sounds ridiculous but it's true")
- Never corporate. Never generic SaaS.

**What Cloverleaf AI sounds like**:
- "If you're waiting for the RFP, you've already lost."
- "Not behind a login. Not in a dashboard they check once and forget."
- "500 Motorola sellers. Zero ops overhead."

**What Cloverleaf AI does NOT sound like**:
- Vague benefit claims: "Empower your team to achieve more"
- Generic SaaS verbs: leverage, streamline, synergize, unlock, harness, enable, optimize (as a buzzword)
- Hollow superlatives: innovative, cutting-edge, best-in-class, game-changing
- Passive constructions that bury the point

---

## Sentence & Structure Style

**Short sentences are a feature, not a bug.**
Intentional fragments are allowed — and often preferred — for emphasis and rhythm. Do not "fix" them.

✅ Correct: "In their inbox. First thing in the morning."
❌ Wrong: "They receive it in their inbox first thing in the morning."

**Mix short punches with longer explanatory sentences.** The pattern is: short hook → longer context → short landing.

**Natural connective flow is also on-brand** — especially in seller-facing or empathy-first copy. Rhythmic parallelism and natural connectors ("and", "not", sequencing verbs) create momentum and feel human. Do not flag these as filler.

✅ "Know the decision makers. Hear pain points in their own words. Listen, learn, and show up as a partner, not a vendor."
✅ "We track decision makers, not dollars. 70,000+ identified speakers. Know who spoke, what they said, and what it means for your deal."
❌ Flagging "listen, learn, and show up" as filler — these are active, purposeful verbs with rhythm and meaning.

**Filler means corporate softeners, not natural connectors.** Filler words are vague, meaningless qualifiers and SaaS buzzwords: "leverage", "empower", "streamline", "unlock", "innovative", "best-in-class". Natural language that serves rhythm or emotional resonance is not filler — it's voice.

**Active voice throughout.** If a sentence can be made active without losing meaning, make it active.

**Lead with the problem.** Name it bluntly before offering the solution. Don't soften the pain.

**Concrete specifics beat vague claims.** If a number exists, use it.
✅ "70,000+ government agencies"
❌ "thousands of government agencies"

## Grammar & Punctuation Rules

**Oxford comma**: Always use it.
✅ "meetings, pain points, and decision makers"
❌ "meetings, pain points and decision makers"

**Em dashes**: Do not use. Replace with:
- A period or line break for a hard stop
- A colon for an explanatory follow-on
- Parentheses if truly parenthetical (use sparingly)

**Contractions**: Allowed and encouraged. They keep the tone human.
✅ "you're", "they're", "it's", "don't", "can't"

**Capitalization**:
- Sentence case by default for headlines and subheads
- Title case is acceptable when design balance requires it — do not change title case in context where it appears intentional
- Product name is always "Cloverleaf AI" (capital C, capital A, no other variations)

**Numbers**: Use numerals for stats and metrics ("70,000+", "$1M", "90%+"). Spell out numbers under ten in running prose.

**Headline punctuation**: Full sentence headlines (subject + verb) take a period. Noun phrase labels with no verb do not.
✅ "If you're waiting for the RFP, you've already lost."
✅ "How It Works"
❌ "How It Works."

**Punctuation in lists/bullets**: Be consistent within a list. If one bullet ends with a period, all should. If none do, none should.

## Audience Assumptions

- The reader knows B2G terminology: RFP, GTM, TAM, patch, front-line sellers, procurement, committee meetings
- Do not explain these terms or soften the jargon
- The reader is skeptical of vendor claims — earn credibility with specifics, not adjectives

---

## Company Name Usage

The company name must always be written as **Cloverleaf AI** — no exceptions.

**Always use:**
- Cloverleaf AI

**Never use:**
- Cloverleaf (without "AI")
- CloverLeaf (wrong capitalisation)
- Clover Leaf (two words)
- Clover Cleaf AI (typo)
- CloverLeaf AI (wrong capitalisation)
- Cloverleafai (no space, no capital A)

When editing copy, scan for all variations and correct them to "Cloverleaf AI" in every instance. This applies to body copy, headlines, subheads, footers, CTAs, and metadata.

---

## Editing Workflow

When editing copy, apply changes in this order:

1. **Voice pass** — Does it sound like Cloverleaf AI? Fix generic SaaS-speak, passive voice, vague claims, and corporate filler.
2. **Structure pass** — Is the rhythm right? Punch up flat sentences. Preserve intentional fragments.
3. **Company name pass** — Find every instance of the company name and ensure it reads "Cloverleaf AI". Correct all variants (Cloverleaf, CloverLeaf, Clover Leaf, CloverLeaf AI, Cloverleafai, etc.).
4. **Grammar & punctuation pass** — Apply Oxford comma, remove em dashes, check capitalization, check contraction usage.
5. **Specificity pass** — Are there vague claims that could be made concrete? Flag them with a note if no data is available to fill them.

Return the edited copy cleanly. If material changes were made to meaning or structure (not just polish), include a brief note at the end explaining the key changes.
